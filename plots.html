data("ny_noaa")

This dataset contains information about the weather in NYC at different points in time, recording information like date, prcp, snow, snwd, tmax, tmin for a total of 2595176 entries.

We first clean this dataset and then use a filtered subset of only observations made in 2007 on the first of the month, so that our visualization isn’t too crowded and therefore understandable.

ny_noaa_78 = ny_noaa |> 
  janitor::clean_names() |> 
  separate(date, 
           into= c("year", "month", "day"), 
           sep="-", 
           remove=TRUE) |> 
  mutate(
    month = as.numeric(month),
    day = as.numeric(day),
    year = as.numeric(year)
  ) |> 
  filter(year == 2007 | year == 2008, day == 1)

From this filtered and cleaned dataset, let’s make a few plots.

The first plot we are going to make is visualizing the average precipitation in NY, grouped by month, comparing 2007 to 2008:

ny_noaa_78 |> 
  group_by(year, month) |> 
  summarize(mean_prcp = mean(prcp, na.rm=TRUE), .groups = "drop") |> 
  mutate(
    year = as.factor(year),
    month = factor(month.abb[month], levels = month.abb)
  ) |> 
  plot_ly(
    x = ~month, 
    y = ~mean_prcp, 
    color = ~year, 
    type = "bar", 
    colors = "viridis"
  )

As you can see from the graph above, the total amount of rainfall in 2008 was greater than the total in 2007. In both years, we can see that the distribution of precipitation has a gradual peak from the months of early winter, peaking in spring and early summer, and then gradually decreasing as the summer goes on. Both distirbutions have single-month spikes in a winter month, December fo 2008 and January for 2007.

The next plot we can show is a boxplot detailing the spread of individual precipitation observations by month in 2007:

ny_noaa_78 |> 
  filter(year == 2007, !is.na(prcp)) |> 
  mutate(
    year = as.factor(year),
    month = factor(month.abb[month], levels = month.abb)
  ) |> 
  plot_ly(
    x = ~month, 
    y = ~prcp, 
    type = "box", 
    colors = "viridis"
  )

In this graph, we can see that the per month distributions, shown by the boxplots, reflect the mean precipitation patterns we previously saw. Note that several months, like February and March, have an extremely low median value, but we saw that the mean was non-zero, so we can infer that the mean is mostly represented by a few heavy rain/snowfall days. Outside of January, one observation in June actually contains the highest level of precipitation from that whole year.
